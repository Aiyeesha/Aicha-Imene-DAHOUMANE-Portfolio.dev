## Context

Tours for Life, a growing travel agency, wanted to digitize its commercial management end to end. The agency's president needed a Salesforce CRM covering the full cycle: prospect acquisition, conversion to travelers, trip management, and sales performance tracking by zone (North / South).

One need surfaced **during scoping**: managing the bus fleet directly in Salesforce, since a single bus can be assigned to several trips. It's the kind of late addition that tests how solid a data model is.

## The data model

| Object | Role | Design choice |
|---|---|---|
| **Prospect** | Standard Lead, tab renamed | Phone required on entry — a prospect with no number isn't workable |
| **Traveler** | `Account` with Person Accounts enabled | Dedicated Record Type "Account to Traveler": lets you **convert a prospect into an individual with no business account** — the normal B2C case |
| **Trip** | New object — the catalog | Destination, departure/arrival, price, status, available seats |
| **Purchased Trip** | New object **distinct from Trip** — the booking | Links Traveler + Trip, luggage, seats purchased, bus number |
| **Bus Fleet** | New object | `lookup` to Trip — one bus, several trips |

The central modeling decision: **separating the catalog (`Trip`) from the transaction (`Purchased Trip`)**. Merging the two would have duplicated trip data on every booking and made a simple "how many seats are left?" impossible. Two objects, one relationship, and capacity becomes a derived value again.

## The no-code automation

Two **Record-Triggered Flows**, no Apex:

- **Flow #1** — confirmation email automatically sent to the prospect when their status changes from "information gathered" to "interested".
- **Flow #2** — automatic decrement of the "Available Seats" field on the Trip whenever a related Purchased Trip is created or updated.

The no-code choice is deliberate: two simple record-triggered rules, no bulk or async processing. **Flow is the right tool here** — Apex would be over-engineering. Knowing where no-code stops matters as much as knowing how to code.

## The zone-based security model

- **2 profiles**: Sales Director (full access), Sales Rep (create/edit prospects, convert them, view travelers and purchased trips).
- **3 hierarchical roles**: Sales Management → North Sales Rep / South Sales Rep — management sees both territories.
- **Per-object OWD**: `Private` on Prospect / Trip, `Public Read-Only` on Bus Fleet, `Controlled by Parent` on Purchased Trip.
- **Sharing Rules by role (North / South)** on Prospect and Trip: territories are kept separate, with read-only visibility on the other zone's travelers.

## The architecture retrospective

With hindsight, the **one profile per level** choice (Director / Sales Rep) isn't the most scalable. A single "Sales Rep" profile + a **Permission Set** granting director-level rights would be more maintainable: Permission Sets stack and can be audited, whereas an extra profile is one more divergence point to keep in sync on every change.

Naming this isn't downplaying the deliverable — it's showing that a decision gets **revisited**. A junior ships and moves on; the difference is going back to the choice.

## Reports

| Report | For whom | What it drives |
|---|---|---|
| Available Trips (Status = Open) | Sales reps | Which offer to pitch, with price / seat totals |
| Prospects to Travelers | Sales + management | Conversion rate and date, by rep and role |
| Trips Purchased per Sales Rep | Management | Performance grouped by role (North / South / Management) |

Reports pinned to the Salesforce home page for daily use.

## Decisions & assumed limitations

| Decision | Alternative rejected | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Person Accounts** for Travelers | A Contact under a generic Account | Travelers are individuals; Person Accounts is Salesforce's B2C model |
| **Separate Trip / Purchased Trip** | A single Trip object carrying the booking | Catalog ≠ transaction: separates stable data from per-booking data |
| **Bus Fleet as a `lookup`** to Trip | Master-detail | A bus outlives the deletion of a trip; no cascade delete wanted |
| **100% Flow automation** | Apex trigger | Two simple record-triggered rules — no-code fits, Apex would be unjustified |

Assumed limitations:

- **Person Accounts is an irreversible org setting.** Enabled here knowingly, but it's a decision that commits the whole org — to validate with the client before, not after.
- **`lookup` on Bus Fleet**: no automatic capacity roll-up summary on the Trip side (unlike the catalog's "available seats" field, driven by Flow). An overbooking check at the bus level would still need adding.
- **Solution demonstrated on sandbox**, not proven at a growing agency's real volume.

## What this project demonstrates

**Sales Cloud fundamentals done properly**: Person Accounts for B2C, a data model that separates catalog and transaction, owned no-code automation where it's enough, a role- and zone-based security model. And an **explicit architecture retrospective** — proof that a choice gets questioned, not forgotten once the deliverable is handed in.
